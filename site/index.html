Beyond Engagement

Designing and stress-testing user-goal-aligned recommendation systems for teen-facing contexts.

This project asks how recommendation systems can respect a person's current goal, context, agency and safety—and how the systems measuring recommendation quality can themselves fail.

Project status

What this work is—and is not

This is independent portfolio research. It is not sponsored by Meta, does not use Meta internal data, and does not describe any platform's production systems.

Evidence boundary: All reported experiments are synthetic offline simulations. No teenagers or other human participants, real user data, production telemetry, or live platform systems were involved. No human evaluation has been conducted. The prototype uses fictional deterministic fixtures.

Research question

How might a recommendation system serve a user's explicitly stated goal while respecting safety constraints, uncertainty, user control, and the reliability limits of its own measurement process?

The project explores a shift from engagement maximization toward context-aware, user-goal-aligned optimization under safety and well-being constraints.

Key lessons from the simulations

  • Optimizing an objective does not guarantee that the objective is correctly understood.
  • Automated evaluators can reward presentation cues without improving underlying synthetic utility.
  • Benchmark coverage, evaluator integrity and monitoring quality are separate problems.
  • Monitoring can be attacked; fallback systems can inherit the same proxy weaknesses.
  • More feedback or online calibration is not automatically better.
  • Safety constraints should not be treated as interchangeable with ordinary relevance scores.

Central conclusion: recommendation quality, user-intent understanding, evaluator integrity, benchmark coverage and monitoring burden must be treated as distinct design and measurement problems.

Methods and limitations

The programme used synthetic candidate items, simulated goals, constructed latent utility functions, scenario-level splits, paired comparisons, counterfactual style manipulations, adversarial test worlds and monitoring simulations. Historical split/provenance limitations are documented in the audit register. A V5.13 result-record conflict remains unresolved and must not be presented as settled.

These findings do not establish real teen preferences, mental-health outcomes, causal benefit, production safety or deployment readiness. The synthetic safety filter is not a real moderation system.

Read the research reports and audit files before citing any result.

Independent adult review

Adult professionals are invited to critique the research framing, experimental claims, rubric, evaluator-invariance tests and prototype usability. Participation is voluntary. Reviewers are not asked to certify safety or endorse the thesis. No reviewer feedback is represented as received until it is actually submitted and documented.

Any future research involving minors would require a separate ethics-reviewed protocol, applicable local approvals, guardian permission where required, teen assent, privacy protections, safeguarding and voluntary withdrawal.
